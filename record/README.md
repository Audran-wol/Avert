- JSON and CSV versions, a media browsing page, source references, duplicate exclusions and a concrete agent handoff.

## Start here

1. Read `FINDINGS.md` for country-by-country results and date caveats.

## The strongest additions

**Cameroon:** September flooding in Douala, including a follow-up published **18 September**; August flooding in Limbe/Kumba with a separate Mbende landslide; and a September seasonal warning. The two Douala date windows (10–11 and 12–13 September) are preserved rather than quietly merged into a fabricated exact date.

**Ghana:** September reporting on Weija flood impacts and Daboya monitoring; June/July location-specific evidence in Afienya, Tse Addo, Totopey, Anlo Afiadenyigba, Weta, Samreboi/Asankragwa and the Central Region. An additional August 2025 Nsawam–Pokuase road gallery fills a historical gap.

I did **not** confirm a flood beginning yesterday, 18 September, in either country. Douala has a report published that day, about earlier flooding. Search and publication coverage are incomplete, so this is not a claim that no other flood occurred.

## Use the images honestly

Open-license images include their original file-page attribution and CC BY-SA 4.0 links. Keep those credits, note resizing, and observe the license for adaptations. Buea/Guéré event-date matching still needs review even though their licenses permit reuse.

Recent press images have `display_ready: false` because publication rights are unverified. A working URL is not permission to rehost a photograph. Open the original report, request permission or use an authorised publisher embed where available. The hackathon context does not itself settle image rights.

No generated flood images were added. If you later create them, store them as visibly labelled **AI-generated scenario illustrations**, never as past-event photographs, satellite detections or model-training ground truth.

## What this does—and does not—improve

These additions improve historical coverage, locality matching, evidence galleries and AI answers grounded in sources. They do not supply measured flood depths, observed polygons or calibrated prediction accuracy. Every new record has null geometry unless a future verified geospatial source supplies it. Photographs alone cannot establish a 90% forecast-accuracy claim.
